## What happened

**[E]** Before a lunch with a customer the user asked whether anything needed preparing, and then
noticed that none of six earlier preparations for that contact had ever asked it. All six listed what
to report -- material to answer with -- and never what the user needed to have **decided** before
walking in.

**[E]** The gap is structural. Step 3 asks what the meeting is about; nothing asks what the user needs
from it. A signal the other party gave in one meeting (*"we are discussing internally whether to
invest"*) went unanswered for weeks and surfaced again at the next.

## The change

**Step 3.5: What does the user need to have thought through?** Before writing, look for:

- a question the other party is likely to open that cannot be answered by reading a file -- direction,
  budget, whether to continue, what the user wants from the relationship;
- an unresolved signal from an earlier meeting -- those land in conversations, not emails;
- a decision on the agenda that commits the user beyond this meeting.

If any applies, **the document opens with it, before what was delivered**: material can be read at the
table, a position cannot be formed there. Two or three questions with the trade-offs named, not an
essay. Look back through earlier preparations for the same contact.

**Pitching is not opening a door.** Telling a customer what to buy is pitching; saying what you see
ahead and asking whether they want to go there together is how continuation gets discussed at all. A
preparation that only warns against the first leaves the user with nothing to say on the second, so
it names an opening line the user could actually use.
